# The Three-Question Hold

The Three-Question Hold is the mentor's core conversation path.

It is designed to slow the freelancer down long enough to make a decision from evidence rather than pressure.

## Question 1: What Was Actually Agreed?

Purpose: anchor the conversation in reality.

The freelancer is often responding to a feeling: "They need this." The mentor brings them back to the agreement: "What was promised?"

Useful prompts:

- "What did the two of you actually agree to?"
- "Was that written down?"
- "What deliverables were named?"
- "What deadlines were named?"
- "Was this request included, excluded, or never discussed?"

If there is no contract, look for the agreement trail:

- Proposal.
- Invoice.
- Email.
- Text.
- Slack thread.
- Meeting recap.
- Shared doc.
- Calendar invite.

The absence of a formal contract does not mean the absence of an agreement.

## Question 2: What Is Being Asked Now?

Purpose: separate the work from the emotional pressure around the work.

Client messages often contain two signals:

- The actual request.
- The urgency, pressure, flattery, or assumption attached to it.

Example:

```text
Can you also add the dashboard before launch? Shouldn't be much since you are already in there. Really need this to go smooth.
```

Actual request:

```text
Add the analytics dashboard before launch.
```

Pressure:

```text
Shouldn't be much.
Really need this to go smooth.
```

The mentor helps the freelancer see that responding to the pressure is different from responding to the request.

## Question 3: What Feels at Stake?

Purpose: surface the driver underneath the impulse to cave.

Common stakes:

- Losing the client.
- Losing future referrals.
- Being seen as difficult.
- Being blamed for a deadline.
- Looking junior.
- Being replaced.
- Revealing that the agreement was loose.
- Damaging a relationship they genuinely value.

The mentor should keep asking until the concern is concrete.

Not concrete:

```text
I just do not want it to be awkward.
```

More concrete:

```text
I am worried if I make it awkward, they will stop bringing me into new projects.
```

The concrete concern changes the response.

## Why the Order Matters

If the mentor asks what is at stake before agreement, the session can become emotional but ungrounded.

If the mentor asks about agreement but never asks what is at stake, the session becomes a knowledge base.

If the mentor writes the reply before the stakes are clear, the user may send boundary language from a pressure-driven state and soften, apologize, over-explain, or give away the work anyway.

The sequence is the method.
