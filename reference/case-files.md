# Case Files

These case files show The Hold across full scenarios: message, diagnosis, fear, response, and debrief.

## Case 1: The Launch Dashboard

### Client Message

```text
Hey, quick one. Before we launch Thursday can you also wire up the analytics dashboard we talked about? Shouldn't be much since you are already in there. Really need this to go smooth. Thanks!
```

### Agreement

Landing page, checkout flow, email integration, and launch QA by Thursday. Analytics dashboard was discussed as a later improvement but not included in the launch scope.

### Diagnosis

The dashboard is not forgotten scope. It is later scope arriving early.

The pressure phrases are:

- "Before we launch Thursday"
- "Shouldn't be much"
- "Really need this to go smooth"

The client is likely anxious about launch. The freelancer is at risk of absorbing that anxiety and turning it into unpaid work.

### Named Fear

```text
I am afraid that if I charge for this, they will think I am difficult and replace me after launch.
```

### Response

```text
Happy to build the analytics dashboard. That is separate from the Thursday launch scope, so the clean move is for me to price it and slot it right after launch. That keeps Thursday focused and gets the dashboard done properly.
```

### Debrief

The response does not reject the client. It protects the launch, names the dashboard as separate, and offers a next step. The fear was replacement, so the message emphasizes care and competence without giving away the work.

## Case 2: The Investor Demo Weekend

### Client Message

```text
We showed the demo internally and there are a few things we really need cleaned up before investors see it Monday. Can you jump on polish this weekend? Mostly visual stuff, should be straightforward.
```

### Agreement

Clickable prototype by Friday. Investor-ready polish was never included. No weekend availability was promised.

### Diagnosis

The client is converting internal feedback into weekend urgency. "Mostly visual stuff" minimizes a batch of work that could expand quickly.

### Named Fear

```text
I am afraid they will think I do not care about the company if I make this billable.
```

### Response

```text
I care about Monday landing well. The polish list is outside the clickable-prototype scope, but I can take it on as a weekend rush pass. Send me the full list by 3 PM and I will price the pass around the items that matter most for the investor demo.
```

### Debrief

The response addresses the real fear directly: seeming uncaring. It shows commitment while making the weekend work explicitly billable.

## Case 3: The Endless Support Thread

### Client Message

```text
Can you fix these three copy things today? Also, going forward, what is the best way for us to send little updates when we need them? It should not be much, just small changes here and there.
```

### Agreement

Website redesign and one week of launch support. Launch support ended last Friday.

### Diagnosis

This is a transition moment. The client may not realize they are asking for ongoing support. If the freelancer handles it casually, the support arrangement may become unpaid and indefinite.

### Named Fear

```text
I am afraid that if I turn this into a support plan, they will feel like I am charging them for every little thing.
```

### Response

```text
I can handle today's copy fixes. Since launch support ended Friday, ongoing updates are best handled as a simple support arrangement instead of one-off messages. I can send two lightweight monthly options so you have an easy path for small changes.
```

### Debrief

The response allows a small bridge without letting the bridge become the new normal. It names the support boundary and offers a practical structure.
